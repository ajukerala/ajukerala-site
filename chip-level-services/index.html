Motherboard repair in Dubai

I have been doing chip-level repair for more than 10 years. In that time I have seen the same thing again and again: a shop tells the customer the motherboard is gone and quotes a new one. When I open the board, the real problem is usually one small part, like a MOSFET, a charging chip or a shorted capacitor.

So I don’t guess. I test the board, find the part that failed and replace that part. If a board really can’t be saved, I will tell you straight, before you spend anything.

I mostly work on HP, Dell, Lenovo, Asus and MacBook laptops, for customers in Dubai and Sharjah.

Send me your laptop model and what it is doing, and I’ll tell you what I think the problem is.

Laptop problems I fix

These are the faults I see most often on my bench.

Laptop not turning on

You press the power button and nothing happens. No light, no fan. This is usually the power section of the board: a MOSFET, a power IC or a short somewhere. I trace the power line until I find it.

Laptop not charging

Charger plugged in, but the battery stays at zero or the light doesn’t come on. Often it is the charging IC or the DC input circuit, not the charger and not the battery.

Short circuit on the board

Sometimes the laptop just won’t start, or the charger light blinks and goes off. That’s a short. I find the shorted part with thermal testing and replace it.

No display, black screen

Fans spin, keyboard lights up, but the screen stays black. Before you buy a new screen, let me check the board. It can be RAM, BIOS or the display circuit.

BIOS problems

Laptop gets stuck on the logo, restarts in a loop or won’t boot after an update. Many of these I can fix by reprogramming the BIOS chip.

Water and liquid damage

Tea, water or juice on the keyboard. Switch it off and bring it quickly. The longer it sits, the more the board corrodes. I clean it and replace the damaged parts.

Why repair the chip and not replace the board?

A new motherboard is expensive, and for older models it can be hard to even find one. Most of the time it is not needed.

  • You pay for the part that failed, not a whole new board.
  • Your laptop keeps its original board.
  • You know exactly what was wrong and what I changed.
  • Older laptops that “can’t be fixed” often can.

Not every board can be saved. Heavy liquid damage or a burnt processor can be the end. If that’s the case, I’ll say so.

Is it worth repairing an old laptop motherboard?

Often, yes. Most faults are one bad part, like a charging chip or a MOSFET, not the whole board. Replacing that part usually costs much less than a new board or a new laptop.
